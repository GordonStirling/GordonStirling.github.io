Adding Value as a Data Practitioner / 03

PURPOSE > DATA

This is part 3 of the Adding Value series, exploring how data practitioners create value in the real world.

The core challenge that we started this series with was the sheer volume and complexity of data available to us and how this can rob us of clarity (Post 1 here). As data practitioners, we naturally see problems and solutions through the bias of our own lens. So when faced with complexity we tend to see a data problem and default to building a data solution. This can lead us to building eloquent, over-engineered solutions to the wrong problems.

How do we make sure we are solving the right problem?

Start with Purpose.

Who are you trying to support?

What problem are you trying to solve?

What are you trying to change or influence?

Who → Problem → Influence

When designing human systems, purpose comes first. This purpose then shapes the functions required for the system to perform (Meadows, 2008). Purpose gives us a focus and a direction. This creates useful constraints (yes constraints are good!) on the volume of data and complexity of analysis we need to consider. In a world where we can measure almost anything, constraints help us decide what deserves our attention.

Critically, purpose provides us with a way to navigate complexity, by focusing our attention on what is important rather than what is available or quantifiable.

How do we identify a clear purpose?

Purpose is not something we invent in our heads, it is something we discover in the environment we are working in. We need to look up and out (into the environment) rather than down and in (into the data) (Dekker, 2011). That starts with observing what is happening, listening to conversations and asking questions. From there, we can make inferences about what matters, where uncertainty exists and where our work might add value. This is human centred design.

The aim is to build contextual awareness by understanding what matters. The more contextual awareness we develop, the clearer the purpose will become and the easier it will be to identify opportunities where our work can add value.
